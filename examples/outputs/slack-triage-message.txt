This file shows what the AI SOC Assistant ACTUALLY posts to Slack today: plain
text, no Markdown, sections separated by a blank line, bullets using the "•"
character. The .json files in this directory are the PLANNED normalized
structure that a future parsing stage would produce (docs/roadmap.md item 2).
Nothing in the current workflow emits JSON.

The sample below corresponds to examples/inputs/wazuh-firewall-alert.json and to
the same event described in suspicious-triage.json. It is illustrative output
written to match the format the system prompts require; a live model will vary
in wording. All addresses are RFC 5737 documentation addresses.

🔥 FortiGate Security Analysis

Verdict: Suspicious

Severity: Medium

Overall Risk: Low

Confidence: Medium

Summary

FortiGate FG-EDGE-01 denied outbound UDP/161 connection attempts from 192.0.2.10
to 192.0.2.20 under policy 784.

The logged sessions recorded zero duration with no transferred bytes or packets.

Evidence

• Timestamp: 2026-08-03T09:14:22Z
• Device: FG-EDGE-01
• Source IP: 192.0.2.10
• Source Port: 54122
• Destination IP: 192.0.2.20
• Destination Port: 161
• Protocol: UDP
• Service: SNMP
• Action: deny
• Policy ID: 784
• Policy Name: TEMP-DENY-LOG
• Sent Bytes: 0
• Received Bytes: 0
• Session Duration: 0

Assessment

The firewall blocked every logged attempt, and the zero byte and packet counters
confirm that no session was established and no data was exchanged.

The repeated pattern against a single service is consistent with two
explanations the supplied evidence cannot separate: a monitoring agent retrying
SNMP polling against a host that no longer permits it, or internal enumeration
of SNMP services.

The policy name TEMP-DENY-LOG indicates a temporary rule, which raises the
likelihood that a legitimate service was caught by a recent policy change.

Overall Risk is assessed as Low because the traffic was denied and no compromise
is demonstrated. The verdict is Suspicious rather than Benign because the
originating process and business purpose have not been established.

The supplied evidence does not demonstrate reconnaissance, exploitation,
malware, command and control, or compromise.

Recommended Actions

• Identify the asset and owner of 192.0.2.10 and confirm whether an SNMP
  monitoring agent is expected to poll 192.0.2.20.
• Review endpoint telemetry on 192.0.2.10 to determine which process initiated
  the UDP/161 traffic.
• Confirm when policy 784 was created and whether it was intended to block this
  traffic.
• Correlate historical firewall logs for the same source and service.

Missing Information

• Asset owner and business role of 192.0.2.10
• Process that initiated the SNMP traffic
• Change record or intent behind policy 784
• Historical baseline for SNMP traffic between these hosts

NOTES FOR REVIEWERS

1. The Wazuh rule carried a MITRE mapping of T1046 (Network Service Discovery).
   It does not appear in the report. That is correct behaviour: the mapping
   describes what the detection rule is for, and the supplied evidence does not
   independently demonstrate that discovery activity occurred.

2. Severity (Medium) and Overall Risk (Low) differ deliberately. Severity
   describes the immediate operational impact of the observed event; Overall
   Risk describes the likelihood that it represents a genuine incident after
   accounting for the fact that the traffic was blocked.

3. The rule fired 14 times. Repetition is reported as an observation, not
   treated as proof of scanning. Repeated blocked connections alone do not
   establish reconnaissance, exploitation, malware, C2 or compromise.

4. The numeric Wazuh rule level does not appear anywhere in the output. The
   prompts require it to be converted to a severity band and never printed.

5. Nothing here is a decision. No block, no isolation, no ticket, no case. The
   analyst who pasted the alert reads this and decides what happens next.
